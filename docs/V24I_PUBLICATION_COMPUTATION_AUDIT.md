# V24-I: publication computation audit

V24-I is the paper-facing audit of the completed primitive K16 search.  It
does not rerun the SAT campaign and does not require a DRAT or LRAT proof for
every leaf.

The workflow downloads the immutable results of V23, V24-B, V24-C, and V24-D,
checks their exact source commits and ledger hashes, and reconstructs every
binary refinement that replaced an earlier `UNKNOWN`.  It then:

1. verifies that the 581 direct V23 closures and all replacement subtrees
   cover exactly the original 676 roots;
2. verifies 1,124 distinct terminal formulas: 1,119 exact solver `UNSAT`
   terminals and five unit-propagation contradictions;
3. checks that there are no terminal `UNKNOWN`, `SAT`, or missing records;
4. rematerializes every terminal CNF and checks its SHA-256;
5. freezes the seven theorem-CNF hashes, four historical ledger hashes,
   workflow run IDs, source commits, solver provenance, and terminal table;
6. emits `publication-audit.json`, `terminal-formulas.csv`, and checksum
   manifests in a single reproducibility artifact.

The stable computational statement is:

```text
K16_PRIMITIVE_676_OF_676_SOLVER_LEVEL_UNSAT_AUDITED
```

This is the appropriate evidence level for a reproducible computer-assisted
mathematics paper.  The partial DRAT/LRAT certificates generated by V24-E/F
remain useful spot checks but are not prerequisites for the statement above.

## Claim boundary

The computation audit establishes the exact closure of the frozen primitive
search.  The paper must still provide human-readable proofs that:

- the seven root boxes are disjoint and exhaustive for the target primitive
  K16 Pisa tournaments;
- the CNF variables and clauses faithfully encode those objects and the Pisa
  inequalities;
- every theorem-level cut used by the CNFs is valid;
- the separate decomposable/weighted-quotient analysis covers the
  non-primitive case.

In particular, a green V24-I workflow must be described as a reproducible
solver-level computation audit, not as a formally verified theorem by itself.
